# Multi-label studies

Reproducible material for the multi-label part of the OPERA paper. All
studies share one solve core:

1. the **deep case study** on the medical dataset (978 clinical free-text
   records, 45 ICD-9-CM labels, 21 auditable labels; 15 confirmatory
   seeds) — Part 1 below;
2. the **generality study** (seven public datasets, 15 seeds each, full
   protocol) and the **real-data scale study** (three large datasets,
   3 seeds each, computational role) — Parts 2 and 3 below;
3. the **staged-certification study**: the closed-form bounds hierarchy
   `U* <= U_mon <= U_B0 <= U_B` and the solver-free pre-certification of
   the whole campaign (Tables 14-15 of the paper) — Part 4;
4. the **hard-instance studies** on Mediamill: the formulation ablation
   and the measured effect of the precedence inequalities of the paper
   (Section 6.6) — Part 5;
5. the **analyses over the archived campaign records** (budget-limited
   root gaps, frontier coverage and the capped cells) — Part 6 — and the
   **figure scripts** — Part 7.

Everything was **pre-specified**: cost structure, seeds, eligibility rule,
baselines, aggregation plan and dataset list were frozen before the
confirmatory runs; the confirmatory seeds derive from a recorded master
entropy (`seeds_config.json`). All ten datasets ship in this repository
under `raw/` and `data/`, together with the frozen sha256 manifest, so no
network access is needed.

This repository ships **inputs and integrity records** (datasets, frozen
seeds and protocols, the development-seed register, hash manifests); the
numbers of record are reported **in the paper**. Every output file is
regenerated by the scripts below, and the section
[Interpreting the output files](#interpreting-the-output-files) explains
how to read each of them.

Every console line you must check is quoted verbatim next to each step.
(Console messages are in English; a few JSON field names inside output files
remain in Portuguese for consistency with the frozen protocol records, and
are translated in the interpretation section at the end.)

## What is audited

For each eligible label `k` (at least 10 positives and 10 negatives in the
corpus), a pool of `m = 20` classifiers records out-of-fold (OOF)
predictions over the whole corpus via stratified 10-fold cross-validation.
Two prediction readings are audited: `M^0.5` (probability threshold 0.5)
and `M^tau*` (the cost-implied threshold, computed per fold from the
training partition). Utilities use a per-label 100-point scale with false
costs scaled by multipliers `(lambda-, lambda+)` in three regimes:
R1 = (2,2) balanced, R2 = (4,2) false negatives dearer, R3 = (2,4) false
positives dearer, plus R4, a balanced random assignment of the three
regimes across labels (frozen seed 20260812; in the generality study the
assignment derives per dataset from
`SeedSequence([20260812, alphabetical_index])`).

The audit is run per label, which is exact by the per-label decomposition
proposition of the paper. Internally every objective is **integer**
(payoffs `(N, P, -lambda- * N, -lambda+ * P)` on the `P*N/50` scale), so
optima compare exactly across solvers and machines; normalised values are
recovered as `U_norm = 50 * U_int / (P*N)`.

Per (label, regime, reading) the battery certifies: the joint optimum `U*`
and its go/no-go verdict, the LP root bound, the consensus-restricted
optimum at `t = 1` (whose gap to `U*` is the consensus premium `G_cons`),
the exact minimum-cardinality optimal committee `|J|_min` (lexicographic
tie-break, a second MILP, no epsilon tricks), three sequential baselines
(A-OR, A-MAJ, B) and, on the `tau*` readings, the complete cost-robustness
frontier `rho*` (Dinkelbach iterations on the false-positive payoff).
Solver settings are frozen: zero MIP gap, time limit 600 s per solve.

| solver | role in the multi-label studies |
|---|---|
| IBM CPLEX 22.1.1 | **solver of record** for the batteries; also provides the instrumented time-to-verdict runs (MIP callbacks) |
| HiGHS (via scipy) | independent verification (must match exactly) and the retraining environment (scikit-learn lives on this side) |

The roles are reversed with respect to the binary study, where HiGHS is
the solver of record; the instrumentation required CPLEX callbacks. Every
optimum is certified by both solvers either way, and the equality
criterion between two batteries is **exact on integers**: `U_int`,
verdict, `G_cons` (integer) and `|J|_min` per (label, regime, reading).
The optimal committee itself may legitimately differ when alternative
optima exist.

## Two ways to run: Docker (the execution of the paper) or bare metal

**Docker is the execution of record.** The images pin the base image by
digest, the Python interpreter by patch version and every package by exact
version; what runs in them is, layer by layer, the same environment that
produced the numbers in the paper. Inside the pinned image on a given
machine, register construction is bit-for-bit deterministic (verified by
rebuilding registers twice and hashing).

**Bare metal also works** and is documented below for every step. Two
caveats, stated honestly:

- The solve layer is exact and integer, so **batteries computed from the
  same register match bit-for-bit** whatever the environment or solver.
- Register **construction** (model training) is floating-point: across
  different CPUs/BLAS builds, trained probabilities can differ in the last
  bits (dominated by k-NN distance ties), so your registers may not
  hash-match the manifests of record even with identical package versions.
  Folds are identical by construction; every scientific conclusion is
  expected to hold. If you need strict fidelity, use Docker.

### Setup A — Docker (recommended)

Build the two binary-study images first (see the root README), then stack
the multi-label layers on top:

```bash
# from the repository root
docker build -f docker/Dockerfile --target highs -t opera-audit:highs .
docker build -f docker/Dockerfile.mlc-highs --build-arg BASE=opera-audit:highs -t opera-mlc-highs .

# CPLEX side (optional if you stay on the free solver; see the licence note)
cp /path/to/your/cplex_studio2211.linux_x86_64.bin docker/cplex_installer.bin
docker build -f docker/Dockerfile --target cplex -t opera-audit:cplex .
docker build -f docker/Dockerfile.mlc-cplex --build-arg BASE=opera-audit:cplex -t opera-mlc-cplex .
```

**CPLEX licence note.** IBM ILOG CPLEX is proprietary software: **you must
supply your own installer** (`cplex_studio2211.linux_x86_64.bin`; IBM
offers it at no cost to academics through its academic initiative). The
installer is git-ignored and deleted from the image after installation.
Never install the `cplex` package from PyPI for this purpose — that is the
Community Edition, capped at 1000 constraints, and it will fail on these
instances. The built `opera-audit:cplex` / `opera-mlc-cplex` images
contain IBM software: never push them to any registry, public or shared.
Only the Dockerfiles are redistributable.

**Free-solver-only note.** Everything scientific reproduces with HiGHS
alone (image `opera-mlc-highs`, no licence needed): every optimum,
verdict, `G_cons`, `|J|_min`, baseline and frontier is certified
identically — that is the point of the integer scale. The only
CPLEX-exclusive outputs are the instrumented trajectories (time-to-verdict
fields) and the indicator-constraint variant of the ablation, which are
reporting extras, not verdicts. Wherever a command below uses
`opera-mlc-cplex` with `--backend cplex`, the free path is the same
command in `opera-mlc-highs` with `--backend highs` and without
`--trajectories`.

A practical Docker note: containers write as root, so files and
directories they create (`data/`, `confirmatory/`, `registers_gen/`, ...)
will be root-owned on the host. If you later want to manage them without
`sudo`, run `sudo chown -R "$USER":"$USER" .` in this directory.

### Setup B — bare metal (no Docker)

Two virtual environments, because the CPLEX 22.1.1 Python API supports at

**Learning + HiGHS environment (required):**


(The last command installs IBM's own `cplex` Python bindings from your
local installation; adjust the path if you installed elsewhere. Do not
`pip install cplex` from PyPI — Community Edition, see above.)

Every step below is given in both forms. The Docker blocks run from this
directory (`experiments/mlc`) and mount it as `/work`; the bare-metal
blocks assume the corresponding venv is active and the working directory
is `experiments/mlc`.

## Step 3: the batteries

Docker (the execution of the paper; several days of machine time; do not
run other heavy load concurrently — the timing discipline is part of the
frozen protocol):

```bash
chmod +x run_gen.sh
nohup ./run_gen.sh > gen_run.log 2>&1 &
tail -f gen_run.log
```

`run_gen.sh` runs, in order: the confirmatory stratum (7 datasets x 15
seeds: register in `opera-mlc-highs`, battery with trajectories in
`opera-mlc-cplex`), the scale stratum (3 giants x 3 seeds, same recipe),
the full HiGHS re-verification of seed 1563301818 on the seven
confirmatory datasets, and the deterministic selection of verification
labels for the giants. Hashes accumulate in
`batteries_gen/manifest_gen.txt`. It is re-runnable: existing files are
skipped; a (dataset, seed) whose register construction fails technically
leaves a `.FAILED` marker and is skipped thereafter (a frozen protocol
state, reported as such — no substitution).

**Running a subset / sharding across machines.** `run_gen.sh` accepts
dataset names as arguments; with no arguments it runs all ten (the
command above). With arguments it runs only those datasets, each with the
seed count of its stratum (15 for the confirmatory seven, 3 for the
giants), including their share of the verification stage:

```bash
./run_gen.sh emotions scene      # two confirmatory datasets, 15 seeds each
./run_gen.sh delicious           # one giant, 3 seeds
```

This is how the campaign can be sharded across several machines — one or
two datasets per machine, each machine a full clone of this repository
with its own images. Three rules keep a sharded campaign sound: keep a
whole dataset on a single machine (every seed of it), so each dataset has
one register-construction environment; record which machine ran which
dataset (the optima and verdicts are machine-independent — that is the
integer-exactness guarantee — but training floats and timings are not);
and compare timings only within one machine. When all shards finish,
collect every shard's `registers_gen/timing_*.json`,
`batteries_gen/battery_*.json`, `batteries_gen/verify_*.json` and
`manifest_gen.txt` into one place — aggregation (Step 5) reads only these
files and can run anywhere.

Bare metal (two-phase equivalent; free-solver users use `--backend highs`
and drop `--trajectories`):


Full re-verification on the seven confirmatory datasets, seed 1563301818
(`run_gen.sh` already does the battery half; the loop below is the
complete bare-metal equivalent plus the exact comparison):

```bash

    python run_battery_gen.py --base $B --backend highs --core \
        --register registers_gen/register_${B}_seed${S1}.npz \

    python compare_results.py batteries_gen/verify_${B}_seed${S1}.json \
        batteries_gen/battery_${B}_seed${S1}.json
done
```

Required last line for each dataset:
`... fields identical => EXACT MATCH  [criterion: core (U_int, verdict, gcons_int)]`
(the count is up to `L_eleg x 18` fields). Cells in which the record's
solver hit the frozen 600 s limit carry bounds, not certificates, so they
are excluded from the exact criterion and checked for **bound coherence**
instead (the side that closed at optimality must be `>=` the side's
incumbent that hit the limit); when present they are counted in the same
line, e.g. `; 3 resource-limited cells excluded, bounds coherent`. A
`BOUND INCOHERENCE` line, by contrast, is a genuine solver disagreement
and fails the comparison.

**Why `--core`.** The verification pass re-solves, per (label, regime,
reading), the two certificate-bearing problems — the joint audit (`U_int`
and the verdict) and the `t = 1` restriction (whose gap is `G_cons`) —
whose null solution is always feasible, so HiGHS always holds an incumbent
under the frozen 600 s limit and either certifies the optimum or reports a
`time_limit` status. The min-`|J|` tie-break is excluded from this pass
for an honest technical reason: its MILP constrains utility to `U >= U*`,
so finding any feasible solution can itself be hard; the CPLEX backend
receives the optimal audit solution as a warm start, but the scipy/HiGHS
interface offers no warm-start mechanism, and on hard instances HiGHS can
reach the time limit with no incumbent at all. `Jmin` therefore carries
the CPLEX certificate, and its machinery is cross-checked independently by
the 200-instance QA of Step 2 (exhaustive enumeration, both backends,
min-`|J|` included) and by the medical study's full 504-field cross-solver
match, in which every `Jmin` solve closed at optimality.

# Part 4 — the staged-certification study (Tables 14-15)

Two scripts, neither of which runs a solver, re-derive how far the
campaign's verdicts can be certified **before** any MILP is built:

- `precert.py` evaluates, per cell, the trivial candidate decisions

# Interpreting the output files

The numbers of record are in the paper; this section explains how to read
what the scripts write, so you can check any of them against your own
runs. All JSON field names are as produced by the scripts (some are in
Portuguese; translations are given below).

## Registers (`register_*.npz`, `confirmatory/`, `registers_gen/`)

NumPy archives with, per eligible label `k`: `probs_<k>` — the `n x 20`
matrix of out-of-fold probabilities (columns follow the frozen pool order
of `pool_frozen.py`); `folds_<k>` — the 10 test folds (row-padded with
-1); plus `eligible` (label indices) and `Y` (the full `n x L` label
matrix). The generality builder also writes
`registers_gen/timing_<dataset>_seed<S>.json` with `T_wall_s` (wall time
of the whole register build), `T_fit_sum_s` (sum of individual fit times
across all folds and configurations), `njobs`, and the register's
`sha256`. A `register_*.FAILED` text file marks a technical training
failure (frozen protocol state; content is the error).

## Batteries (`battery_*.json`, `verify_*.json`)

`meta` records the dataset/backend/register/R4 seed and total wall time.
`labels` maps each eligible label id to:

- `P`, `N`: positives and negatives of the label in the corpus;
- six blocks named `R1_m05`, `R1_mtau`, `R2_m05`, `R2_mtau`, `R3_m05`,
  `R3_mtau` — regime x reading. Each block:
  - `U_int`, `U`: the certified optimum, integer scale and normalised
    100-point scale (`U = 50*U_int/(P*N)`); `U0_int`: utility of the null
    decision (flag nobody); `dU`: `U - U0` normalised;
  - `verdict`: `"go"` iff `U_int > 0`, else `"no-go"` — the certificate;
  - `t`: optimal consensus threshold; `J_desc`: committee size found by
    the joint audit; `Jmin`: certified minimum committee size among optima
    (the lexicographic tie-break); `fp`, `fn`: false counts of the optimal
    decision;
  - `gcons_int`, `gcons`: consensus premium `U* - U*(t=1)` (integer /
    normalised) — how much the optimal threshold beats the OR rule;
  - `gap_AOR_int`, `gap_AMAJ_int`, `gap_B_int`: integer gaps of the three
    sequential baselines to `U*` (0 = the baseline happens to be optimal);
  - `root_bound`, `root_gap_abs`: LP-relaxation bound at the root and its
    normalised distance to `U*`;
  - `profiles`, `compress`: distinct (prediction-profile, class) columns
    after aggregation, and `n/profiles` — the compression that makes the
    MILPs small;
  - `t_build`, `t_solver`, `t_end_to_end`: seconds (per-solve timing);
    `nodes`: branch-and-bound nodes; `status`: `"optimal"` when every
    solve of the block closed at certified optimality, or
    `"time_limit(n)"` when `n` solves of the block hit the frozen 600 s
    limit and returned their best incumbent instead — in that case the
    affected values are bounds, not certificates, and the cell is treated
    as resource-limited per the frozen protocol (the min-`|J|` solve is
    warm-started with the optimal audit solution, so it always has an
    incumbent). On the CPLEX side a solver ending with no feasible
    solution at all raises a clean error and the cell is recorded as a
    technical failure. On the HiGHS side (used for verification), a solve
    that reaches the 600 s limit before finding any incumbent falls back
    to the trivial feasible solution (signal nothing, `U = U0`) and the
    block status gains the suffix `;trivial` — the cell is then
    resource-limited like any other time-limited cell, is excluded from
    the exact-match criterion by `compare_results.py`, and keeps bound
    coherence (any certified optimum is `>=` the trivial `U0`). This can
    happen on the hardest cells of the giants (e.g. *mediamill*),
    where CPLEX always carries an incumbent but the HiGHS interface has
    no warm start;
  - CPLEX with `--trajectories` only: `T_verdict` (seconds until the
    incumbent/bound already certified the sign of `U*`, i.e. the verdict,
    before optimality), `T_delta`, `traj_points` (incumbent/bound
    trajectory);
  - `mtau` blocks only: `frontier` with `case` = `"finito"` (finite;
    `rho` = the factor by which the false-positive cost can be multiplied
    before `U*` reaches 0, `solves` = Dinkelbach iterations),
    `"inf"`/`"inf_degenerado"` (unbounded: positive utility with zero
    false positives is achievable), or `"sem_regiao_viavel"` (no viable
    region: `U*(rho=0) <= 0`);
- `R4_profile`, `R4_source`: the `(lambda-, lambda+)` pair assigned to
  this label by the frozen R4 permutation and the regime it copies —
  R4 blocks are read off the corresponding `R<i>_mtau` entries by
  composition, with no new solve.

## Medical aggregate (`agg_final15.json` from Step 5)

`n_seeds`; `per_seed` — one row per battery file with, per regime x
reading: `U_bar` (mean normalised `U*` over the 21 labels), `U_sum`,
`GO`/`local_nogo` (label counts by verdict), `dU_bar`, `gcons_pos` (labels
with positive consensus premium), `global_verdict` (sign of `U_sum`) and
`rq1_hit` (true iff the aggregate says go while at least one label is
locally no-go — the coexistence phenomenon of the paper), plus
`R<i>_flips` (labels whose verdict differs between the 0.5 and `tau*`
readings); `summary` — median/IQR/min/max across seeds of each of those
statistics, plus `*.rq1_freq` and `*.global_go_freq` (seed counts);
`per_label` — per label: flip frequency across seeds, no-go frequency and
median/IQR of `U` per regime x reading, and the R1 frontier profile
(`R1_frontier_finite`, `R1_frontier_inf`, `R1_rho_med`).

## Generality aggregates (`agg_<dataset>.json`)

`summary` per regime x reading: `estados_FA` — seed counts of the three
aggregate states (`go_sem_conflito` = go without local conflict,
`conflito` = aggregate go with at least one local no-go, `global_nogo` =
aggregate no-go); `U_bar`, `local_nogo`, `q_cons`, `q_AMAJ`, `q_B` — each
as median/min/max/IQR across seeds, where the `q_*` are per-seed
**shares** of eligible labels with positive consensus premium / positive
gap over A-MAJ / positive gap over B. Per regime: `R<i>_flips_pos`
(no-go -> go flips from the 0.5 to the `tau*` reading), `R<i>_flips_neg`
(the opposite direction), `R<i>_rank_pos` (median normalised prevalence
rank of the flipped labels; 0 = rarest, 1 = most prevalent),
`R<i>_rho_med` (median finite frontier) and `R<i>_p_inf` (share of labels
with unbounded frontier). `per_seed` carries the same quantities seed by
seed, and `rank_prevalencia` the normalised prevalence rank of every
eligible label.

## Supplements

`ablation_*.json`: per (label, reading), one block per formulation
(`V1_row`, `V2_agg`, `V3_enum_t` and, on CPLEX, `V4_indicator`), each
with `U_int` and its size/time metrics (`t_build`, `t_solver`,
`t_end_to_end`, variable/constraint counts, nodes, root bound); the run's
final line prints `divergences=<count>` (`U_int` disagreements between
formulations), which must be 0. `grid_*.json`: per label, `U_int`/verdict
for the 9 payoff pairs `(lambda-, lambda+) in {2,4,8}^2` on the fixed
`M^0.5` reading. `r4_supplement.json`: one row per permutation
(`perm` 0 = the battery's own assignment, checked against it via
`perm0_confere_com_bateria: true`), each with the per-reading `U_sum`,
`U_bar`, `U_min`, `GO` count, the flip count between readings
(`flips_05_tau`) and the full label assignment — all recomposed from the
already-solved R1-R3 oracles. `compare_results.py` prints per-field
divergences if any, reports which criterion applied — `full` (4 fields
including `Jmin`) when both inputs are full batteries, `core` (`U_int`,
`verdict`, `gcons_int`) when either input is a `--core` verification
battery — treats cells whose `status` records a time limit on either side
as resource-limited (excluded from the exact criterion, checked for bound
coherence and counted in the final line), and exits with status 0 only on
`EXACT MATCH` (usable in CI). A legacy note: batteries produced before the
root-bound status fix may carry `node limit exceeded` as a block status;
it marks exactly the cells whose main audit solve hit the 600 s limit (the
root-node status used to overwrite the sticky record), and it is treated
as `time_limit` everywhere.

## Pre-certification and hierarchy files (`precert_*.json`, `hierarchy_*.json`)

`precert_*.json`: `meta` (register, battery, candidate list, total time);
`labels` maps each label to its six cells, each with `best_cand` (the best
trivial candidate: name, `U_int`, `t`, `J`), `ub_simple_int` (the
reachability upper bound), `pre_verdict` (`go_pre` / `nogo_pre` / `open`)
and, when a battery was given, `pli` (the certified optimum it is checked
against). The run summary counts `cells_total`, `go_pre`, `nogo_pre`,
`open`, how the open cells were later closed by the solver
(`open_pli_go` / `open_pli_nogo`), and `disagreements` — which must be an
empty list. `ratios_best_over_Ustar` summarises, on certified go cells,
how close the best candidate comes to the optimum (`frac_at_optimum` is
the share where it already is one).

`hierarchy_*.json`: per cell, `U_B_int`, `U_B0_int`, `U_mon_int` (the
three closed-form bounds), `profiles_voto` (distinct vote profiles),
`nogo_mon` (true iff `U_mon <= 0` — the bound alone certifies the no-go)
and the timing. The run summary counts, per bound, how many certified
no-gos the bound closes on its own, and `cadeia_violacoes` (chain
violations against the certified optima) — which must be empty.

## Precedence experiment (`r1_mediamill_R2.json`)

# Frozen design (what you may not vary and still call it a replication)

- Eligibility: `P >= 10` and `N >= 10` per label; stratified 10-fold CV.
- Pool: the 20 configurations of `pool_frozen.py`; random states derived
  per (seed, label, config) via `SeedSequence` as recorded in
  `seeds_config.json`; continuous features (per the frozen census
  attribute-type rule) get a StandardScaler fitted inside each training
  fold.
- Costs: the 100-point per-label scale; R1 (2,2), R2 (4,2), R3 (2,4); R4
  balanced assignment with the frozen derivation. Zero MIP gap, TL 600 s.
- The 15 confirmatory seeds (master entropy 20260816) and the 3 scale
  seeds (its first three); development seed 20260811 excluded by design.
- The dataset list itself (frozen before acquisition), the corpus-union
  rule, the census-before-solve discipline and the CAL500
  continuous-label binarisation caveat documented in the paper.
